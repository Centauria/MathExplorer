## Statement

Determine all pairs of positive integers $(n, m)$ satisfying the Diophantine equation $n! + 1 = m^2$, where $n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdots n$ denotes the factorial. The only known solutions are $(n, m) = (4, 5)$, $(5, 11)$, and $(7, 71)$; such pairs are called Brown numbers. The open problem is to prove that these three pairs are the only solutions, or to exhibit a fourth one. Known partial results: Berndt and Galway verified by computation that no other solution exists for $n \le 10^9$, and Overholt (1993) showed that the ABC conjecture implies there are only finitely many solutions.

## Context

A one-line equation mixing the factorial and square functions whose solution set resists all known Diophantine machinery; it is a benchmark for how little we can prove about factorials near squares, and progress would likely require new ideas applicable to other exponential-polynomial equations.

## Known partial results

Search on 2026-08-07: Wikipedia and a February 2026 arXiv posting (arXiv:2004.09256v2, 'Results of Brocard-Ramanujan problem on diophantine equation n!+1=m^2') both explicitly call the problem unsolved; MathWorld confirms only three solutions are known.
