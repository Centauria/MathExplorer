## Statement

Grimm's conjecture (1969): if $n+1, n+2, \dots, n+k$ are $k$ consecutive composite positive integers, then there exist $k$ distinct primes $p_1, \dots, p_k$ such that $p_i$ divides $n+i$ for every $1 \le i \le k$. In other words, every run of consecutive composite numbers admits an injective assignment of prime divisors, one distinct prime per composite. Partial results: Erd\H{o}s and Selfridge proved the conjecture for infinitely many maximal composite runs between consecutive primes, and Laishram--Shorey and Laishram--Murty established it under various size and smoothness assumptions; the general case remains open.

## Context

An innocent-looking statement about the combinatorics of prime factorizations in short intervals; it encodes deep information about the distribution of small prime factors and is connected to prime-gap conjectures of Legendre type, yet its formulation needs nothing beyond divisibility.

## Known partial results

Search on 2026-08-07: the surveyed literature (Laishram--Murty, 'Grimm's Conjecture and Smooth Numbers'; Laishram's IJNT paper) states and treats Grimm's conjecture as open; the only claimed proof found is an unrefereed 2017 viXra posting, not an accepted solution.
